Cover story

The portfolio you can scuba dive

A game developer from Florida built his homepage as an ocean. The deeper you scroll, the stranger it gets. We put on a wetsuit.

Fig. 1. The Sunlight Zone, 0 m. The depth meter in the top bar counts down as you scroll. Fish not to scale.

03Cover story

Most portfolios are a grid of thumbnails. This one is a dive. You start at the surface, where a name floats over kelp and coral, and every project waits further down, filed like a specimen with its own dossier. There is The Living World, a fantasy MMO built for a group of friends, and an anime autobattler, and the strange little tools that made them.

Then there's the car. Up in the meadow sits a small pixel car, and clicking it drives you off the page into a painted forest, brushed stroke by stroke, where trees bend from the roots and the paint you smear across the air dries while you watch.

“A portfolio that rewards you for poking it.”

Fig. 2. Click the car; drive into the forest. Fig. 3. Projects are filed like specimens.

Investigation

889 links. One page. No database.

Somewhere in Florida is a library with no building. The Hub holds 889 tools, fonts, effects and design references, every one of them actually gone through, then sorted into a single page you can search before your coffee cools.

Readers can save favorites into their own lists, flip the whole thing between styles like “Midnight archive”, and suggest a find of their own. A second tab holds documents you can read right there.

Dashboard confidential

One page that watches everything

A private stats page that runs as its own tiny Cloudflare Worker, behind a login only the editor gets through. Portfolio visits, itch.io downloads and GitHub traffic sit side by side. Every night at 1 AM it saves a snapshot, because GitHub forgets traffic after 14 days and itch.io only keeps running totals. Cost to run: zero dollars.

README remix

Sharks on the profile page

GitHub profiles are usually a list of badges. This one opens with ASCII sharks drawn in kana characters, borrowed from the editor's live-wallpaper ocean, Abyssal. Click them and you dive straight into the portfolio.
